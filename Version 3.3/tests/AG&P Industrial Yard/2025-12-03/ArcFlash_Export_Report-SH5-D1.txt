🔥 STEP 2: INCIDENT ENERGY CALCULATION
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

📊 IEEE 1584-2018 LOW VOLTAGE METHOD
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Voltage Range: 208V - 600V

Arc Power Calculation:
  log₁₀(Iarc) = log₁₀(9318) = 3.9693
  log₁₀(E) = k + 0.662×log(Iarc) + 0.0966×V + ...
  log₁₀(E) = 3.2768
  Arc Power = 10^3.2768 = 1891.64 W

Incident Energy Formula:
  E = 4.184 × Carc × t × (610²) / (D² × 10000)
  Where:
    Carc = Arc power = 1891.64 W
    t = Exposure time = 0.033 sec
    D = Working distance = 457 mm
    610 = Normalization constant (mm)

  E = 4.184 × 1891.64 × 0.033 × 372100 / (457² × 10000)
  E = 0.05 cal/cm²

Incident Energy at 18":  0.05 cal/cm²

📏 STEP 3: ARC FLASH BOUNDARY
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The arc flash boundary is the distance at which the incident energy
equals 1.2 cal/cm² (onset of second-degree burn).

Boundary Calculation:
  Target Energy (Eb):      1.2 cal/cm² (second-degree burn threshold)
  Incident Energy (E):     0.05 cal/cm² at 18"
  Working Distance (Dw):   18 inches

  Formula: Db = Dw × √(E / Eb)
  Db = 18 × √(0.05 / 1.2)
  Db = 18 × 0.1978
  Db = 3.56 inches

                         90 mm (0.09 meters)
